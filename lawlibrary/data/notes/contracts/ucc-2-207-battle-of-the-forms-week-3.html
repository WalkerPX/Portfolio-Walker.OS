Big picture

  • Common law "mirror image" rule: acceptance must match offer exactly. Any additional/different term = counteroffer, not acceptance.
  • UCC rejects mirror image — doesn't fit modern commercial deals (pre-printed POs and acknowledgment forms, fine print nobody reads).
  • § 2-207 keeps a contract alive despite mismatched forms, then decides what happens to the variant terms.
  • Three-question framework: (1) contract formed? (2) what happens to variant terms? (3) if writings don't form a contract but conduct does, § 2-207(3).

Doctrine outline

  • § 2-207(1) — formation. Definite, seasonable expression of acceptance operates as acceptance even with additional/different terms — unless acceptance is expressly conditioned on assent to those terms.
  • § 2-207(2) — additional terms are proposals.
    • Either party a non-merchant: variant terms drop out unless expressly accepted.
    • Both merchants: variant terms become part of the contract unless (a) offer limited acceptance to its own terms, (b) terms materially alter the deal, or (c) offeror objects within a reasonable time.
  • § 2-207(3) — contract by conduct. Contract can arise from conduct recognizing a deal exists even if forms don't match. Terms = what the writings agree on + supplementary terms (course of performance, course of dealing, trade usage, Code gap-fillers).
  • Classic scenario: seller's acknowledgment contains terms additional to/different from buyer's purchase order.

Tests and elements

  • § 2-207(1) acceptance test: unequivocal + timely expression of acceptance = acceptance, not counteroffer, even with variant terms — UNLESS expressly conditioned on assent to variant terms.
  • § 2-207(2) merchant checklist — term drops out if ANY:
    • Offer limited acceptance to its own terms.
    • Term materially alters the contract.
    • Offeror objects within a reasonable time.
  • Material alteration (Official Comment 4): incorporating term without express awareness would cause surprise or hardship. Examples: negating warranties, limiting remedies, departing from trade usage.
  • Non-merchant rule: variant terms are mere proposals — drop out unless expressly accepted.
  • § 2-207(3) elements: writings don't establish a contract + parties' conduct does → terms = agreed terms in writings + UCC gap-fillers.

Cases

  • Princess Cruises, Inc. v. General Electric Co.
    • Facts: Princess PO for routine maintenance on cruise ship. GE erred; ship unavailable; lost profits. Princess sued for breach of contract, breach of warranties, negligence; sought lost profits as consequential damages.
    • GE conceded breach but argued its acceptance excluded consequential damages and capped liability at contract price (~$230,000) vs. $4.5 million jury award.
    • § 2-207 stakes: under common law, GE's liability-limiting acceptance = counteroffer; later conduct (ship delivered, work performed) would manifest assent to GE's terms. Under § 2-207, Princess's PO sets baseline; GE's limitation joins only if it doesn't materially alter the deal.
    • Pivotal question: common law or Article 2? Determines whose terms govern and whether GE's cap survives.
    • Holding: not stated on slides — now briefed: King v. Trustees of Boston University.

Exam angles

  • Always run the three questions in order: (1) formation, (2) variant terms, (3) conduct.
  • Check merchant status of each party first — it flips the § 2-207(2) analysis (proposal vs. automatic inclusion).
  • Check for express conditioning language in the acceptance — knocks out § 2-207(1).
  • Run the three § 2-207(2) exceptions as a checklist; material alteration is the usual battleground.
  • Use Comment 4 examples (negating warranties, limiting remedies, departing from trade usage) to argue materiality.
  • Threshold issue in every fact pattern: does Article 2 apply at all (goods vs. services/mixed)? Outcome determines whose terms govern (Princess Cruises).
  • Liability caps and consequential-damages exclusions are recurring variant terms — test them for material alteration.
  • If forms don't form a contract, pivot to § 2-207(3): conduct + agreed terms + gap-fillers.
