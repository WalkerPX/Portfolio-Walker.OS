Quick reference

Citation: 307 N.J. Super. 461, 704 A.2d 1321 (App. Div. 1998) · Casebook: Knapp p. 272 · Topic: Promissory Estoppel › Reliance in negotiations One line: A casino's repeated assurances that a lease deal was "done" could support promissory estoppel for a tenant who gave up its old lease in reliance. New Jersey no longer requires a "clear and definite" promise.

Overview

A modern case applying promissory estoppel to preliminary negotiations. It relaxes the traditional requirement of a "clear and definite promise" and focuses on whether reliance was reasonable and injustice would otherwise result.

Parties

  • Plaintiff / appellant: Pop's Cones, Inc. A TCBY yogurt franchisee operating in Margate, New Jersey. It wanted reliance damages after giving up its location.
  • Defendant / respondent: Resorts International Hotel, Inc. An Atlantic City casino-hotel that negotiated to lease Pop's a boardwalk-front space, then backed out.

Facts

  • Pop's negotiated to relocate to retail space in Resorts' hotel on the Boardwalk.
  • Resorts' representative repeatedly assured Pop's that the deal was essentially complete. Slides: "the deal is done." [Outside knowledge: a Resorts executive reportedly said they were "95% there" and that Pop's should "pack up the Margate store."]
  • In reliance, Pop's declined to renew its existing Margate lease, paid deposits and ordered equipment. It put its equipment in storage.
  • Resorts never signed. It ultimately withdrew and gave the space to a TCBY franchise operated through Host Marriott (Note 4, slides).

Procedural History

  • The trial court granted summary judgment for Resorts, finding no clear and definite promise.
  • Appellate Division: reversed and remanded for trial on promissory estoppel.

Issue

Does promissory estoppel require a "clear and definite promise," and could Resorts' assurances during negotiations support a claim?

Holding

New Jersey no longer strictly requires a "clear and definite" promise. A jury could find Resorts' assurances sufficient, and that Pop's reasonably relied to its detriment. Summary judgment was improper.

Reasoning

  • Traditional elements: (1) a clear and definite promise; (2) made with the expectation of reliance; (3) reasonable reliance; (4) definite and substantial detriment.
  • Modern trend (and Restatement § 90): courts are less rigid about "clear and definite" and look at whether enforcement is needed to avoid injustice, given the parties' relationship and reliance.
  • Resorts' assurances, its knowledge of Pop's lease renewal deadline, and its encouragement to proceed could reasonably have induced Pop's reliance.
  • Pop's sought only reliance damages (lost lease and moving costs), which fits a case where the contract was never finalized.

Rule

Promissory estoppel may be based on assurances made during negotiations, even if they are not a "clear and definite" promise in the classical sense, where the promisor should reasonably expect reliance, the promisee reasonably relies to its detriment, and injustice can be avoided only by enforcement. Remedies may be limited to reliance damages.

Why It Matters

  • It extends promissory estoppel from offers (Drennan) to negotiations that have not produced an offer.
  • Note 1: many jurisdictions still require a "clear and definite promise." Know your jurisdiction.
  • Note 2: a weaker case than Drennan (ongoing negotiations, no formal bid), but reliance was far more substantial.
  • Note 4: expectation damages were provable, since Resorts later leased to a TCBY franchise on similar terms.
  • Note 5: a paradigm case for a duty to negotiate in good faith (Knapp's theory). All material terms were agreed and Resorts walked away without reason. Compare the concurrence in Quake Construction v. American Airlines.

Other Key Points

  • Hypo 3, "It's Basically Yours" (slides): the landlord says "the space is basically yours," and the tenant spends $22,000 on fit-out and declines renewal. Analyze the standard, compare Pop's, and choose reliance or expectation.
  • Related: Walser v. Toyota Motor Sales (reliance damages in promissory estoppel); Reliance and Restitution Damages.
