Big picture

  • Charitable subscriptions can be enforceable without proof of reliance under § 90(2).
  • Reliance still matters in practice: courts factor it in even under § 90(2).
  • Under § 90(2), the burden may shift to the defendant to prove reliance is absent.
  • § 90(2) eliminates the need to prove reliance, but courts are unlikely to ignore it entirely (Note 2).

Doctrine outline

  • § 90(2): charitable subscriptions may be enforceable without proof of reliance.
  • Reliance still weighed: even under § 90(2), courts factor in reliance.
  • Burden shift: the burden may shift to the defendant to prove reliance's absence.
  • Practical limit (Note 2): § 90(2) removes the proof requirement, but courts are unlikely to ignore reliance entirely.

Tests and elements

  • § 90(2) enforcement checklist:
    • Charitable subscription at issue.

    • Note 2: courts unlikely to ignore reliance entirely.

Cases

  • King v. Trustees of Boston University (p. 225) — Charitable Subscriptions.
    • Facts: Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. transferred his personal papers to Boston University, expressing intent that they remain there permanently.
    • Issue: enforceable promise or revocable gift?
    • BU relied by undertaking care, storage, and indexing of the papers.
    • Court applies § 90(2): charitable subscriptions may be enforceable without proof of reliance.
    • Even under § 90(2), courts factor in reliance — but the burden may shift to the defendant to prove its absence.
