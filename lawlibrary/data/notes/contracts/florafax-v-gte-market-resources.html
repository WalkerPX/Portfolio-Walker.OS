Quick reference

Citation: 933 P.2d 282 (Okla. 1997) · Casebook: Knapp p. 929 · Topic: Limits on Damages One line: A telemarketing vendor whose understaffing cost its client a lucrative third-party contract was liable for two years of the client's lost net profits on that contract. They were foreseeable, proved with reasonable certainty, and caused by the breach.

Overview

A contemporary illustration of all the limits on lost-profit recovery together: foreseeability (Hadley), certainty, causation, and net vs. gross profits.

Parties

  • Plaintiff / appellee: Florafax International, Inc. A floral order clearinghouse. It wanted lost profits from its contract with Bellerose (a third party).
  • Defendant / appellant: GTE Market Resources, Inc. (GTE). A provider of telecommunications and telemarketing services. It argued the lost collateral-contract profits were unforeseeable, speculative and not caused by its breach.

Facts

  • Florafax contracted with GTE to provide call-center services so that Florafax could serve its contracts with third parties, including Bellerose.
  • The Florafax-Bellerose contract was signed two weeks before the Florafax-GTE contract, and GTE knew it would be serving Florafax's clients.
  • GTE failed to provide enough telemarketing representatives, especially over the Mother's Day weekend in 1990. Bellerose cancelled its contract with Florafax.
  • The Bellerose contract had a 60-day termination clause.

Procedural History

  • The jury awarded Florafax lost profits over two years on the Bellerose contract. [Outside knowledge: about $750,000.]
  • Supreme Court of Oklahoma: the trial court judgment is affirmed as to the lost-profit award. Three justices dissented.

Issue

May a party recover lost profits on a collateral contract with a third party, lost because of the defendant's breach, and if so, subject to what limits?

Holding

Yes. The lost Bellerose profits were (1) within the parties' contemplation at contracting, (2) proved with reasonable certainty, and (3) caused by GTE's breach, despite the 60-day termination clause. Recovery is of net, not gross, profit.

Reasoning

  • Foreseeability (Hadley): GTE knew it would serve Florafax's clients, and the Bellerose deal predated the GTE contract. The losses were within contemplation.
  • Note 1: the result probably would not differ if the Bellerose contract had been signed after the GTE contract. Hadley covers losses within contemplation, even from later transactions.
  • Certainty: a long-term relationship with Bellerose and its actual loss provided substantial evidence of the fact of damage. The amount need only be reasonably certain.
  • Causation and the termination clause: termination clauses are common. Absent evidence Bellerose was likely to terminate anyway (the facts showed the opposite), the clause did not cut off damages at 60 days.
  • Net profits: the plaintiff gets a position as good as, not better than, full performance. Gross income would ignore costs avoided.

Rule

Lost profits, including on collateral contracts, are recoverable if they were foreseeable at contracting, caused by the breach, and proved with reasonable certainty. The measure is net profit (Restatement §§ 351, 352).

Why It Matters

  • Restatement § 352 (slides): damages are not recoverable beyond an amount the evidence permits to be established with reasonable certainty.
  • Note 2: clauses limiting liability for consequential damages are far more common than clauses assuming it (compare Princess Cruises v. General Electric).
  • Note 3: a new business or speculative future contracts make certainty much harder. Florafax had an existing contract.
