Quick reference

Citation: 18 Cal. 3d 660, 134 Cal. Rptr. 815, 557 P.2d 106 (1976) · Casebook: cited in Watts v. Watts (Knapp p. 307), Notes pp. 310-314 · Topic: Restitution and Promissory Restitution One line: Courts should enforce express contracts between unmarried cohabitants (except to the extent based on sexual services) and may use implied contract and equitable remedies to protect their expectations.

Coverage

Not reprinted. It is discussed in Watts and Watts Note 1. Facts and procedure marked [Outside knowledge] are not in your materials.

Overview

The leading case on contract and restitution claims between non-marital partners (Watts Note 1, slides).

Parties

  • Plaintiff: Michelle Triola Marvin [Outside knowledge]. She lived with the actor for about six years and wanted half the property acquired during the relationship, plus support.
  • Defendant: Lee Marvin [Outside knowledge]. A film actor who held the property in his name.

Facts

[Outside knowledge] The plaintiff alleged an oral agreement: the two would live together, combine their efforts and earnings, and share equally in property acquired, and she would give up her career as an entertainer to serve as his companion, homemaker and cook, in exchange for his support for life. They separated after about six years, and he stopped supporting her.

Procedural History

[Outside knowledge] The trial court granted judgment on the pleadings for the defendant. The California Supreme Court reversed. (On remand, the plaintiff ultimately recovered little. A rehabilitative award was later reversed on appeal.)

Issue

Are agreements between non-marital partners enforceable, and what remedies are available absent an express agreement?

Holding

[Outside knowledge, consistent with Knapp's description] Express agreements between non-marital partners are enforceable except to the extent they rest on meretricious sexual services. Absent an express contract, courts may look to the parties' conduct for an implied contract, and may use quantum meruit and other equitable remedies.

Reasoning

  • From slides (Watts Note 1): three arguments against relief were all rejected: (1) the Family Code (the marital property scheme doesn't bar cohabitant contracts), (2) legislative preemption, (3) public policy.
  • [Outside knowledge] The court reasoned that changing social norms made it unfair to deny relief, and that sexual services can be severed from other consideration (homemaking, career sacrifice).

Rule

Contracts between non-marital partners are enforceable unless explicitly founded on sexual services. Courts may also find implied-in-fact agreements and award restitutionary relief.

Why It Matters

  • It is the foundation for Watts v. Watts and the cohabitation cases in Knapp.
  • Lesson (Watts Note 2): an apparent legal victory may produce little practical benefit.

Other Key Points

  • Knapp p. 307 cites Marvin in Watts for courts refusing to extend marital property statutes to cohabitants, while allowing contract and equity claims.
