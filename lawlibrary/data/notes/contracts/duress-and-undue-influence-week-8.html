Big picture

  • Two defenses to contract formation: duress and undue influence.
  • Duress = destroying free will by force, threat of force, or bodily harm.
  • Physical duress → contract VOID. Economic duress → contract VOIDABLE.
  • Undue influence = unfair persuasion of a susceptible person, judged by totality.
  • Duress requires a wrongful threat; undue influence does not require bad faith.
  • Modern trend: economic duress and undue influence are fact-intensive, totality-based inquiries.

Doctrine outline

Duress

  • Definition: act of destroying someone's free will by force, threat of force, or bodily harm.
  • Contract entered under duress is void or voidable.
  • Physical duress: gun to the head, threat of force → contract VOID.
  • Economic duress (modern doctrine): free will undermined by unfair pressure short of physical compulsion → contract VOIDABLE.
  • Physical duress → VOID from the beginning; complete lack of volitional conduct means no assent was ever given.
  • Threats of physical compulsion (as opposed to actual physical compulsion) do not strictly render the contract void, but may make assent ineffective depending on the facts.

Undue influence

  • Restatement elements: inducement, unfair persuasion, undue susceptibility, inequitable result.
  • Confidential relationship not required under Restatement, but there must be a sufficient relationship so the person is "justified in assuming that person will not act in a manner inconsistent with his welfare." Restatement (Second) § 177(1).
  • Odorizzi factors are not all required; absence of some factors is not conclusive — look at the totality.
  • Lack of good faith is NOT an element of undue influence.
  • Duress may be harder to prove than undue influence because duress requires a showing of a wrongful threat.

Tests and elements

Totem — three elements of economic duress (checklist):

  • (1) A wrongful or improper threat (need not be illegal; may be enough that it was made in bad faith).
  • (2) Absence of any reasonable alternative to submission to the threat.
  • (3) The threat actually induces the making of the contract.

Undue influence — Restatement elements (checklist):

  • (1) Inducement (causation — is the unfair persuasion the cause of the person's assent?).
  • (2) Unfair persuasion (Odorizzi factors):
    • Unusual, inappropriate time or place.
    • Insistent demand that business be finished at once.
    • Emphasizing untoward consequences of delay.
    • Use of multiple persuaders by dominant side.
    • Absence of third-party advisers.
    • Statement that there is no time to consult third parties.
  • (3) Undue susceptibility to domination (weakness of mind; poor physical, emotional, or mental health).
  • (4) An inequitable result (economic consequences; victim's divergence from prior course; inequity in value of exchange).

Cases

  • Totem Marine Tug & Barge, Inc. v. Alyeska Pipeline Service Co. (p. 596) — establishes the three elements of economic duress under modern law (wrongful/improper threat; no reasonable alternative; threat induces contract).
  • Odorizzi v. Bloomfield School District (p. 606) — resignation by public school teacher accused of criminal homosexual conduct; whether it can be set aside for undue influence. Court first considers duress, menace, fraud, mistake and explains why they do or do not apply. Model of careful doctrinal analysis. (Holding not stated on slides — now briefed: Odorizzi v. Bloomfield School District.)
  • Martinez-Gonzalez v. Elkhorn Packing Co. LLC (p. 615) — migrant farm workers signed arbitration agreement at end of long workday in hotel parking lot, in a language many did not fully understand, without adequate explanation. Majority: elements of duress and undue influence under California law not established. Dissent: majority ignored the "coercive atmosphere" deliberately created by Elkhorn.

Examples and hypos

Hypo 2 — "Take It or Leave It" (Totem facts)

  • Totem completes $300,000 of pipeline work; barge sinks due to Alyeska's alleged failure to maintain pumping system.
  • Totem, facing bankruptcy, demands payment. Alyeska offers $97,500 as "full and final settlement" and implies it will tie up payment in litigation for years if Totem refuses.
  • Totem signs under financial pressure and later seeks to void the settlement.
  • Discussion: three elements of economic duress? Is Alyeska's threat "wrongful"? Why does bad faith matter? Did Totem have any reasonable alternative?

Hypo 3 — "Sign Here — It's Just Paperwork"

  • Staffing company recruits farm workers from another state; 1,800-mile bus trip.
  • Arrive exhausted at 9 p.m.; representative hands stack of documents: "These are just standard onboarding forms. Sign here."
  • One document is an arbitration agreement waiving jury trial. No translation provided.
  • Workers who hesitate told: "If you don't sign, you can't start work tomorrow." Several sign.
  • Discussion: apply Totem's three elements — what result? Apply Odorizzi's elements — what result? What could the company have done differently?

Exam angles

  • Distinguish physical vs. economic duress: VOID vs. VOIDABLE.
  • Threats of physical compulsion ≠ actual physical compulsion — assent may still be ineffective depending on facts.
  • Run the Totem three-element checklist in order; bad faith can supply wrongfulness even if threat is not illegal.
  • "Reasonable alternative" is a separate element — analyze it explicitly.
  • For undue influence, run all four Restatement elements; then weigh Odorizzi factors under totality (not all required).
  • No confidential relationship required — only a relationship justifying reliance under § 177(1).
  • No good-faith requirement for undue influence — honest motives don't defeat the defense.
  • Compare duress vs. undue influence: duress needs wrongful threat; undue influence does not.
  • Martinez-Gonzalez: formalistic element-check (majority) vs. coercive-atmosphere approach (dissent); note the "could the employer have done more" question.
  • Hard cases: strong arguments on both sides; judges can differ on largely uncontested facts.
