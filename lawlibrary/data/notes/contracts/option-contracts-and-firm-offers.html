In one paragraph

Ordinary offers are freely revocable before acceptance, even if they state a deadline (Normile v. Miller). An offer becomes irrevocable when (1) the offeree gives consideration for the promise to keep it open, an option contract (Berryman v. Kmoch: the consideration must actually be paid); (2) § 87(1)(a): a signed writing recites a purported consideration and proposes a fair exchange; (3) UCC § 2-205: a merchant's signed firm offer; (4) § 87(2): foreseeable, substantial reliance (Drennan v. Star Paving); or (5) § 45: the offeree begins invited performance.

Option contracts

  • Definition (slides): an option arises when the offeror's power of revocation is bargained away for consideration. It lets a party investigate a deal without fear that delay will forfeit its ability to accept. The offeror keeps the consideration even if the option lapses.
  • Consideration must be real, not just recited (Berryman). Nominal consideration works if actually paid (Note 2). It need not be money (Note 3).
  • Courts are lenient on nominal consideration for options compared with substantial exchanges. Some imply a promise to pay a recited sum. Berryman rejects that.
  • Restatement § 87(1)(a): an offer is binding as an option if it is in a signed writing, recites a purported consideration, and proposes an exchange on fair terms within a reasonable time (Berryman Note 4, not followed in Kansas).
  • Seal: in some states a sealed option is irrevocable (Note 5).
  • Acceptance of an option is effective on receipt, so the mailbox rule doesn't apply (Note 6).
  • An option is not terminated by rejection or counteroffer, or by revocation, during its term. [Outside knowledge: § 37.]

Firm offers (UCC § 2-205)

  • An offer by a merchant to buy or sell goods in a signed writing that by its terms gives assurance it will be held open is irrevocable without consideration for the stated time (or a reasonable time), not exceeding three months. If the term is on the offeree's form, it must be separately signed by the offeror. [Outside knowledge: the details beyond "merchant firm offer" in the slides.]

Part performance of a unilateral offer (§ 45)

  • Once the offeree begins the invited performance, an option contract arises (Cook v. Coldwell Banker).
