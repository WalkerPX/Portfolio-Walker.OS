Quick reference

Citation: 249 Mont. 282, 815 P.2d 1135 (1991) · Casebook: Knapp p. 454 · Topic: Parol Evidence Rule › Exceptions One line: A subcontractor that signed a fixed-price ($97,500) earthwork contract could not introduce evidence of oral assurances about the quantity, because that evidence directly contradicted the writing. A strong dissent objected.

Overview

A "particularly troubling" application of the parol evidence rule (slides). Even classical courts recognize a fraud exception, but Sherrodd reads it restrictively: evidence of fraud is excluded when it directly contradicts the written terms.

Parties

  • Plaintiff / appellant: Sherrodd, Inc. A family-owned Montana earthwork subcontractor. It wanted to recover for the excess earthwork (on fraud and other theories).
  • Defendants / respondents: Morrison-Knudsen Co. and Schlekeway Construction (general contractors), COP Construction (Sherrodd's direct contractor) and Safeco (COP's payment bond surety).

Facts

  • The project was 50 family housing units in Forsyth, Montana, for the Army Corps of Engineers. Sherrodd subcontracted with COP for earth-moving.
  • While inspecting the site to bid, William Sherrodd was told by a Morrison-Knudsen representative that there were 25,000 cubic yards of excavation.
  • Sherrodd signed a contract to perform all the earthwork for a fixed sum of $97,500, allegedly relying on representations that the parties would work out payment for the actual quantity if it ran over (slides).
  • The earthwork turned out to be about twice the amount called for.

Procedural History

  • The district court (Yellowstone County) granted summary judgment for the defendants.
  • Montana Supreme Court: affirmed. Justice Trieweiler dissented (joined by one other justice). [Outside knowledge: the dissenter's name.]

Issue

Does the fraud exception to the parol evidence rule allow evidence of oral misrepresentations that directly contradict the terms of a fully integrated written contract?

Holding

No. Evidence of an alleged oral promise or representation that directly contradicts the written fixed-price term is barred. The fraud exception does not apply where the claimed fraud consists of a promise inconsistent with the writing. Summary judgment affirmed.

Reasoning

  • The written contract unambiguously required Sherrodd to do all the earthwork for $97,500.
  • Allowing evidence of a contrary oral understanding would vary or contradict the integrated writing, which is exactly what the parol evidence rule forbids.
  • The court treats the fraud exception narrowly, available for fraud that does not contradict the writing (e.g., fraud in the execution, or misrepresentations about collateral matters).
  • Sherrodd also did not properly raise certain theories below (per the pretrial order).

Rule

(Restrictive view) Under the parol evidence rule, evidence of fraud is not admissible where the alleged fraudulent representation directly contradicts the express terms of the integrated writing.

Why It Matters

  • It is the minority, restrictive approach to the fraud exception. Most courts and the Restatement allow evidence of fraud to show a contract is voidable, even when it contradicts the writing ([Outside knowledge: Restatement (Second) § 214(d)]).
  • Dissent (slides): this approach lets a party make explicit misrepresentations and still be shielded if the other side signs a writing saying no representations were made. "This is deeply troubling."
  • It links to Misrepresentation and Nondisclosure: the parol evidence rule can block a defense that would otherwise make the contract voidable.

Other Key Points

  • Hypo 5 (slides), earthwork: apply the majority and the dissent.
  • Practice point: a subcontractor should insist on a quantity-based price, a written estimate, or an express adjustment clause.
