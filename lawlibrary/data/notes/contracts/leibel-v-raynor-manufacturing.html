Quick reference

Citation: 571 S.W.2d 640 (Ky. Ct. App. 1978) · Casebook: Knapp p. 490 · Topics: Good Faith and Fair Dealing, UCC Article 2 - Scope and Formation One line: A manufacturer that ends an indefinite-duration oral distributorship for goods must give reasonable notice under UCC § 2-309(3).

Overview

An implied term case under the UCC. Contracts of indefinite duration may be terminated at will, but § 2-309(3) requires reasonable notification before termination, so the other party can recoup investment and find substitute arrangements.

Parties

  • Plaintiff / appellant: Leibel. An exclusive area distributor of Raynor's garage doors under an oral agreement. He wanted damages for termination without notice.
  • Defendant / appellee: Raynor Manufacturing Co. The garage-door manufacturer. It terminated the distributorship and appointed a new distributor in Leibel's territory.

Facts

  • Leibel distributed Raynor's garage doors under an oral agreement of indefinite duration. [Outside knowledge: he invested in facilities and inventory in reliance on the relationship, and the relationship had lasted several years.]
  • Raynor terminated the arrangement without notice, appointing a new distributor in the same territory (slides).

Procedural History

  • The trial court ruled for Raynor ([Outside knowledge: it held the UCC did not apply to the distributorship and no notice was required]).
  • Kentucky Court of Appeals: the judgment is vacated and the case remanded for further proceedings.

Issue

Does the UCC govern a distributorship for goods, and if so, must the manufacturer give reasonable notice before terminating an indefinite-duration agreement?

Holding

Yes. The UCC applies to the distributorship (a sale of goods predominates), and § 2-309(3) implies an obligation of reasonable notice before termination. Whether Raynor's notice was reasonable is for the factfinder on remand.

Reasoning

  • § 2-309(2): a contract of indefinite duration is valid for a reasonable time, but terminable at will by either party unless otherwise agreed.
  • § 2-309(3): termination (other than on an agreed event) "requires that reasonable notification be received by the other party," and an agreement dispensing with notification is invalid if its operation would be unconscionable.
  • Purpose: to give the dealer time to seek a substitute arrangement and recoup investment, a matter of fairness (slides).

Rule

A party terminating an ongoing sale-of-goods relationship of indefinite duration must give reasonable notification (UCC § 2-309(3)), unless termination occurs on an agreed event or the parties validly agreed otherwise and the waiver is not unconscionable.

Why It Matters

  • Key contrast with Wood (slides): Wood implies reasonable efforts to carry out the parties' intent. Leibel implies reasonable notice as a matter of fairness.
  • It shows the implied-term branch of good faith (Seidenberg category 1) and the UCC's statutory gap-fillers.
  • Note 5: an express "no notice" clause is enforceable unless unconscionable. Immediate termination on an agreed event (e.g., two consecutive years of declining sales) is also effective.

Other Key Points

  • Exam use: franchise and distributorship terminations. Ask: Is it goods? Is the term indefinite? Was there notice? Was there an agreed termination event? Is a no-notice clause unconscionable?
