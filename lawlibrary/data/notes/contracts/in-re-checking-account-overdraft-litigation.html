Unconscionability can be a sword, not just a shield.

Coverage

Known only from Knapp's De La Torre Note 4 and the slides. Details beyond Knapp are [Outside knowledge].

Overview

A departure from the traditional view that unconscionability is only a defense to enforcement.

Parties

  • Plaintiffs: bank customers in multidistrict litigation. They wanted to recover overdraft fees.
  • Defendants: banks that charged the fees. They argued unconscionability could be used only defensively.

Facts

  • From Knapp: the banks allegedly charged customers' accounts excessive overdraft fees and retained the funds. [Outside knowledge: the practices included re-ordering debit transactions from highest to lowest to maximize overdraft fees.]

Procedural History

From Knapp: the court rejected the banks' argument ([Outside knowledge: on motions to dismiss in the MDL]).

Issue

May a plaintiff assert unconscionability as the basis for an affirmative claim to recover money already paid?

Holding

Yes. The court rejected the argument that unconscionability could be used only defensively.

Reasoning

  • From Knapp: if unconscionability could not support a cause of action to recover the improper fees, customers would have no remedy for the banks' unconscionable conduct, because the banks had already taken the money by debiting accounts. There was nothing left for the customers to "defend" against.
  • Compare Langemeier v. National Oats (UCC § 2-302 lets a court enforce a contract without the unconscionable term, which may lead to affirmative relief).

Rule

In some jurisdictions, unconscionability may ground an affirmative claim for restitution of amounts paid under an unconscionable term, at least where a defensive use would leave the victim without a remedy.

Why It Matters

  • Slides (De La Torre Note 4): most courts limit unconscionability to a defense. A handful, including this one, allow affirmative recovery. "Whether it will spread remains to be seen."
  • Knapp cites Brady Williams, Unconscionability as a Sword, 107 Calif. L. Rev. 2015 (2019).
