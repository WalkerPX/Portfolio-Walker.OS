Only the essentials

Rules, elements, tests and key cases. Fuller versions: Contracts Final Exam Outline → Contracts Study Guide (Long). Map: Contracts Law Library (Index). Items marked [Outside knowledge] are not from the course materials.

Order of attack

Law? (goods → UCC) → Formed? → Enforceable? (SOF, defenses) → Terms? → Breach / excuse? → Remedy? Argue in the alternative: contract → PE → restitution.

Formation

  • Objective theory: a reasonable person's reading of outward words and conduct. Lucy v. Zehmer, Leonard v. Pepsico, Ray v. Eurice Bros (duty to read).
  • Offer: a reasonable offeree would think assent closes the deal. Ads are invitations unless definite and leave nothing open (Lonergan v. Scolnick; Izadi v. Machado Ford).
  • Acceptance: mirror image (common law); mailbox rule (effective on dispatch). A counteroffer = rejection.
  • Termination: rejection, lapse, death, revocation (including indirect: Normile v. Miller).
  • Unilateral contracts: acceptance by performance. Beginning performance makes the offer irrevocable (§ 45): Cook v. Coldwell Banker, Sateriale v. RJ Reynolds.
  • Definiteness: no agreement to agree on price (Walker v. Keith). Letter of intent: did the parties intend to be bound? (Quake Construction v. American Airlines).
  • Topics: Mutual Assent and Offer and Acceptance, Agreements to Agree and Letters of Intent.

Consideration (§ 71)

  • Bargained for (sought and given in exchange) or a legal detriment (Hamer v. Sidway).
  • Gift condition ≠ consideration. Test: did the promisor want the act? (Pennsy Supply v. American Ash Recycling yes; Kirksey v. Kirksey no).
  • No: recitals or motive (Dougherty v. Salt); past consideration or moral obligation (Plowman v. Indian Refining, Mills v. Wyman); illusory promises.
  • Adequacy isn't reviewed (Batsakis v. Demotsis; Harris v. Time).
  • Implied duties save "illusory" deals (Wood v. Lucy Lady Duff-Gordon). Continued at-will work = consideration (Marshall Durbin Food v. Baker).
  • Topic: Consideration.

Promissory estoppel (§ 90)

Promise + reasonably expected reliance + actual reliance + injustice without enforcement. Remedy limited as justice requires (usually reliance).

  • Charitable pledges: § 90(2), no reliance needed (King v. Trustees of Boston University).
  • Negotiations: Pops Cones v. Resorts International.
  • Sub bids: Drennan v. Star Paving (§ 87(2), irrevocable once relied on) vs. James Baird v. Gimbel Bros.
  • Options need consideration (Berryman v. Kmoch); UCC firm offer (§ 2-205): a merchant's signed writing, ≤ 3 months.
  • Topics: Promissory Estoppel, Option Contracts and Firm Offers.

Restitution

  • Unjust enrichment = benefit conferred + unjust to retain. No recovery for volunteers.
  • Emergency services (Credit Bureau Enterprises v. Pelo); sub v. owner requires exhausted remedies + the owner paid no one (Commerce Partnership v. Equity Contracting); cohabitants (Watts v. Watts, Marvin v. Marvin).
  • § 86: a promise for a benefit already received is binding to prevent injustice, except a gift or a disproportionate promise (Webb v. McGowin vs. Mills v. Wyman).
  • Topic: Restitution and Promissory Restitution.

UCC Article 2

  • Goods = movable things. Mixed deals → predominant purpose (Jannusch v. Naffziger goods; Princess Cruises v. General Electric services).
  • § 2-204: formation by conduct, open terms OK if intended (Jannusch v. Naffziger vs. EC Styberg Engineering v. Eaton).
  • § 2-207: (1) an expression of acceptance counts unless expressly conditional; (2) merchants: additional terms come in unless limited, material alteration, or objection; different terms → knockout [Outside knowledge]; (3) conduct → agreed terms + gap-fillers.
  • Topic: UCC Article 2 - Scope and Formation.

Good faith

  • In every contract (§ 205; UCC § 1-304). Implied reasonable efforts (Wood v. Lucy Lady Duff-Gordon); reasonable notice (Leibel v. Raynor Manufacturing); discretion limited (Locke v. Warner Bros); Seidenberg's three categories (Seidenberg v. Summit Bank). Doesn't override express terms.
  • Topic: Good Faith and Fair Dealing.

Statute of frauds

  • Within? Suretyship, marriage, land, can't be performed within 1 year (any possibility → outside), goods ≥ $500.
  • Writing: subject, contract made, essential terms, signed by the party to be charged; documents may be combined (Crabtree v. Elizabeth Arden).
  • Exceptions: part performance for land (possession + improvements; Beaver v. Brumlow, Burns v. McCormick); § 139 PE (Alaska Democratic Party v. Rice); UCC § 2-201: a quantity term; merchant's confirmation (10 days), specially manufactured goods, admission, payment or acceptance (to that extent) (Buffaloe v. Hart).
  • Topic: Statute of Frauds.

Parol evidence and interpretation

  • Integrated → no contradiction. Fully integrated → no supplementing either. Consistent terms OK if partially integrated (separate consideration or naturally omitted).
  • Four corners (Thompson v. Libby) vs. Corbin / reasonably susceptible (Taylor v. State Farm, Shay v. Aldrich).
  • Always admissible: interpretation, fraud (but not directly contradicting terms: Sherrodd v. Morrison-Knudsen), duress, mistake, condition precedent, later modification, trade usage (§ 1-303).
  • Topics: Parol Evidence Rule, Interpretation.

Conditions and breach

  • Express condition: strict compliance; substantial performance doesn't count (Oppenheimer v. Oppenheim Appel Dixon, Maxton Builders v. Lo Galbo). Doubtful terms read as promises (§ 227).
  • Excuse: waiver (non-material), prevention, forfeiture = forfeiture + no prejudice + venial inattention (JNA Realty v. Cross Bay Chelsea).
  • Constructive conditions → substantial performance (Jacob and Youngs v. Kent). Factors: purpose, good faith, cost to cure, adequacy of damages.
  • Partial (damages only) → material (suspend, allow cure) → total (terminate) (Sackett v. Spindler).
  • Repudiation: definite and unequivocal; a request to renegotiate isn't one; retraction is possible until reliance, cancellation or a declaration that it's final (Truman L. Flatt and Sons v. Schupf).
  • § 2-609: reasonable insecurity → written demand → no assurance within ≤ 30 days = repudiation (Hornell Brewing v. Spry).
  • Topics: Express Conditions, Material Breach and Substantial Performance, Anticipatory Repudiation and Adequate Assurance.

Remedies

  • Expectation (§ 347): loss in value + other loss − cost avoided − loss avoided.
    • Land: contract price − market value at breach (Crabbys v. Hamilton).
    • Employee quits: replacement cost (Handicapped Childrens Education Board v. Lukaszewski).
    • Construction: cost of completion unless wasteful → diminution (Jacob and Youngs v. Kent).
  • Limits:
    • Foreseeability: natural, or special circumstances communicated at formation (Hadley v. Baxendale, Florafax v. GTE Market Resources).
    • Certainty: net profits.
    • Mitigation (Rockingham County v. Luten Bridge); employment: comparable work, employer's burden (Maness v. Collins).
    • No attorney fees (American rule; Zapata Hermanos v. Hearthside Baking).
    • No emotional distress unless it is the essence of the contract (Erlich v. Menezes).
  • Reliance: out-of-pocket; when profits are uncertain or for PE (Wartzman v. Hightower Productions, Walser v. Toyota Motor Sales).
  • Restitution: quantum meruit, even on a losing contract (US ex rel Coastal Steel v. Algernon Blair); a breacher gets benefit − harm (§ 374; Lancellotti v. Thomas); not where an express term governs (Shaffer v. George Washington University).
  • Specific performance: damages inadequate + equities + definite and supervisable (City Stores v. Ammerman). Never personal services (Reier Broadcasting v. Kramer).
  • Liquidated damages: a reasonable forecast of hard-to-estimate harm, not a penalty. Single look (majority) vs. second look. No mitigation if valid.
  • Topics: Expectation Damages, Limits on Damages, Reliance and Restitution Damages, Specific Performance, Liquidated Damages.
