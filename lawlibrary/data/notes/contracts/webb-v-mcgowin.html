App. 82, 168 So. 196 (1935), cert. denied, 232 Ala. 374, 168 So. 199 (1936) · Casebook: Knapp p. 322 · Topic: Restitution and Promissory Restitution › Promissory restitution (Restatement 86) One line: A man who was saved from death or serious injury by a worker who was crippled in the act, and who then promised the worker lifetime payments, was bound by that promise because he received a material benefit.

Overview

The modern view of promises for past benefits, the model for Restatement (Second) § 86, "promissory restitution." A promise made in recognition of a material benefit previously received is binding to the extent necessary to prevent injustice.

Parties

  • Plaintiff / appellant: Joe Webb. A lumber mill employee who was permanently crippled saving McGowin. He wanted the promised payments to continue.
  • Defendant / appellee: the executors of J. Greeley McGowin's estate. They stopped paying after McGowin's death and argued the promise lacked consideration.

Facts

  • August 3, 1925: Webb was clearing the upper floor of a mill by dropping a 75-pound pine block to the ground below. As he was about to release it, he saw McGowin directly below.
  • To keep it from striking McGowin, Webb held onto the block and fell with it, diverting its course. McGowin was spared. Webb was badly crippled for life.
  • September 1925: McGowin, "in consideration of" Webb having saved him, promised to pay Webb $15 every two weeks for the rest of Webb's life. He paid until his death (1925 to 1934). His estate then refused to continue.

Procedural History

  • The trial court sustained the defendants' demurrer, and Webb took a nonsuit.
  • Alabama Court of Appeals: reversed and remanded. The Alabama Supreme Court denied certiorari.

Issue

Is a promise to pay for a past, unrequested service that conferred a material benefit on the promisor (saving his life or body) enforceable?

Holding

Yes. McGowin received a material benefit, which created a moral obligation sufficient to support his subsequent promise. The demurrer should have been overruled.

Reasoning

  • Saving McGowin from death or grievous bodily harm was a material benefit to him. "Life and preservation of the body" have material, pecuniary value.
  • Where the promisor received a material benefit, his moral obligation to compensate is sufficient consideration for a later promise to pay. Even though the services were unrequested, the promisor's later promise is treated as ratification, as if requested.
  • McGowin's years of payments confirmed the promise was considered and serious, not an emotional impulse.
  • Distinguishing Mills: in Mills v. Wyman, the father received no material benefit. McGowin personally survived because of Webb's sacrifice.

Rule

A promise made in recognition of a material benefit previously conferred on the promisor by the promisee is enforceable, even without prior request, to the extent necessary to prevent injustice. Codified as Restatement (Second) § 86.

Why It Matters

  • It is the leading promissory restitution case. § 86(1): "A promise made in recognition of a benefit previously received by the promisor from the promisee is binding to the extent necessary to prevent injustice."
  • § 86(2) limits: not binding (a) if the promisee conferred the benefit as a gift or the promisor has not otherwise been unjustly enriched, or (b) to the extent the value is disproportionate to the benefit.
  • Comment f: promissory restitution does not apply where the benefit was conferred under a pre-existing contract.

Other Key Points

  • Note 1 (Henderson): "promissory restitution" is more precise than "moral obligation." § 86 asks whether the promisor "has not been unjustly enriched." Webb and Harrington are reconciled because McGowin's nine years of payments showed genuine commitment, not an emotional reaction.
  • Note 4: Webb is taught despite representing a minority rule, as an outstanding tool for teaching analysis and competing approaches.
  • Hypo 10 (slides): a rescuer and a later promise. Apply § 86(2): was it a gift? Is the amount disproportionate?
