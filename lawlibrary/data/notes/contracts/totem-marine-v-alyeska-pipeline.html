Citation: 584 P.2d 15 (Alaska 1978) · Casebook: Knapp p. 596 · Topic: Duress and Undue Influence › Economic duress One line: A small, financially desperate contractor that settled a $260,000-$300,000 claim for $97,500 stated a claim for economic duress. Alyeska allegedly withheld payment knowing Totem faced bankruptcy.

Overview

The leading course case on economic duress. It states the modern elements: (1) a wrongful or improper threat, (2) no reasonable alternative, and (3) the threat actually induced the agreement. Economic duress makes a contract voidable.

Parties

  • Plaintiffs / appellants: Totem Marine Tug & Barge, Inc. (a closely held Alaska corporation that began operating in March 1975), Richard Stair (its vice-president, who personally lent it money and guaranteed its debts) and Pacific, Inc. (Stair's company). They wanted to rescind the settlement and release and recover the full amount owed.
  • Defendants / appellees: Alyeska Pipeline Service Co. et al. The pipeline consortium that hired Totem. It relied on the release.

Facts

  • June 1975: Totem contracted to carry pipeline materials from Houston to southern Alaska. It was Totem's first contract. It chartered a barge and tug with loans and guarantees from Stair and Pacific.
  • Problems arose immediately. Instead of about 2,000 tons of regular pipe, Totem found 6,700-7,200 tons of coated pipe, beams and valves, improperly piled. Loading took 30 days instead of 3. With Alyeska's verbal consent, Totem chartered a second tug, but Alyeska delayed the written amendment.
  • September 1975: Alyeska terminated the contract. Totem submitted invoices of between $260,000 and $300,000.
  • Alyeska delayed payment. Totem told Alyeska it faced bankruptcy without prompt payment. Alyeska allegedly knew this and offered $97,500, "take it or leave it."
  • November 6, 1975: Totem, through Stair, accepted $97,500 and signed a release of all claims.

Procedural History

  • Totem sued to rescind the release and recover the balance. The superior court granted summary judgment for Alyeska.
  • Supreme Court of Alaska: reversed and remanded for trial.

Issue

Can a party rescind a settlement and release on the ground of economic duress, where the other party allegedly withheld undisputed sums knowing the party faced financial ruin?

Holding

Yes, potentially. Totem's allegations, if proven, state a claim for economic duress. There were genuine issues of material fact, so summary judgment was improper.

Reasoning

  • Elements of duress (adopting the modern test):
    1. one party involuntarily accepted the other's terms (assent induced by the threat);
    2. circumstances permitted no other alternative (no reasonable alternative);
    3. those circumstances resulted from coercive acts of the opposite party (a wrongful or improper threat).
  • Wrongful threat: the threat need not be illegal. Withholding payment of an acknowledged debt in bad faith, knowing the creditor's financial straits, to force a lower settlement can be wrongful.
  • No reasonable alternative: suing for the full amount was not adequate, since Totem would have gone bankrupt first.
  • Mere hard bargaining or the stress of business is not enough. The pressure must be wrongful and leave no reasonable alternative.
  • The presence of Totem's attorney at the settlement does not by itself defeat duress, but evidence on this point will matter at trial.

Rule

A contract (including a settlement and release) is voidable for economic duress if a party's assent was induced by an improper threat that left the victim no reasonable alternative. Bad-faith withholding of money owed, knowing the victim's financial vulnerability, can be an improper threat (Restatement (Second) §§ 175-176).

Why It Matters

  • It is the standard for economic duress (slides). Note 2: the threat need not be illegal, and bad faith may suffice. Note 5: no reasonable alternative. Note 6: actual inducement.
  • Note 1: physical duress (actual physical compulsion) makes a contract void, since no assent was given. Economic duress makes it voidable. Threats of physical compulsion may make assent ineffective depending on the facts.

Other Key Points

  • Hypo 2, "Take It or Leave It" (slides): a Totem-style variation with $300,000 of work and a $97,500 "full and final settlement." Apply the three elements.
  • Compare Odorizzi v. Bloomfield School District (undue influence needs no wrongful threat) and Martinez-Gonzalez v. Elkhorn Packing (duress and undue influence claims rejected).
