The short version

A deal you agreed to only because of an improper threat, or because someone you trusted or depended on pressured you unfairly, can be cancelled.

Written for readers with no legal background. For the law-school version, see Duress and Undue Influence.

What it's about

Agreement only counts if it's real. Two doctrines deal with agreement that was forced:

Duress

  • Physical duress (a literal gun to the head) means there was no contract at all (void).
  • Economic duress makes the contract voidable. You generally need:
    1. an improper threat (not necessarily illegal; bad-faith pressure can count),
    2. no reasonable alternative but to give in, and
    3. the threat actually caused you to agree.
  • Hard bargaining is not duress. Taking advantage of a desperate situation you created can be.

Undue influence

  • Excessive, unfair persuasion of someone who is vulnerable to it: tired, ill, grieving, or under the other person's control or trust.
  • No threat is needed. Warning signs include pressure to decide immediately, at an odd time or place, with no chance to get advice, often from several persuaders at once.

An everyday example

A company owes a small contractor $250,000 and knows the contractor will go bankrupt without it. The company says, "Take $90,000 today and sign this release, or wait months for anything." If the company withheld money it knew it owed just to force a cheap settlement, the release may be voidable for economic duress.

The cases, in plain words

  • Totem Marine v. Alyeska Pipeline: a desperate contractor settled a claim of $260,000-$300,000 for $97,500 after Alyeska allegedly held back payment knowing Totem faced bankruptcy. That could be economic duress.
  • Odorizzi v. Bloomfield School District: officials came to a teacher's home right after his arrest, when he was exhausted, and pressed him to resign on the spot. That stated a claim for undue influence, though not duress.
  • Martinez-Gonzalez v. Elkhorn Packing: a farmworker who signed arbitration agreements during an end-of-day orientation didn't prove duress or undue influence, over a strong dissent.

Words you'll hear

  • Void vs. voidable: void is no contract at all; voidable is a contract the victim may cancel.
  • Release: an agreement giving up legal claims, often in exchange for a settlement.
