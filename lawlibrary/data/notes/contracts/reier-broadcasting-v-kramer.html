Quick reference

Citation: 316 Mont. 301, 72 P.3d 944 (2003) · Casebook: Knapp p. 1079 · Topic: Specific Performance › Personal services contracts One line: A radio station could not get an injunction to stop Montana State's football coach from broadcasting for a competitor in breach of his exclusivity clause, because equity will not enforce personal service contracts, even negatively, in these circumstances.

Overview

The course case on equitable relief for personal service contracts. Courts traditionally refuse affirmative specific performance (involuntary servitude, difficulty of supervision), and negative injunctions (barring work for others) are limited.

Parties

  • Plaintiff / appellant: Reier Broadcasting Company, Inc. It owns radio stations in Gallatin County and, until 2002, held exclusive rights to broadcast Montana State University (MSU) athletics. It wanted an injunction barring Kramer from performing for a competing station.
  • Defendant / respondent: Michael Kramer, MSU's head football coach.

Facts

  • January 2001: at MSU's behest, Reier and Kramer signed an employment contract. Reier paid Kramer $10,020/year for exclusive broadcast rights with him. Kramer would appear on the weekly one-hour "Cat Chat" program during football season and record commercials for Reier's advertisers. The contract ran until November 2004.
  • Exclusivity clause (Section Two): Kramer "shall not perform on or permit his name to be used in connection with any other radio or television station or program ... without prior approval and consent in writing by the Station."
  • 2002: MSU awarded its broadcast rights to a competing broadcaster, and Kramer was to perform for it. [Outside knowledge: MSU's contract with the new broadcaster required the coach's participation.]

Procedural History

  • The district court (Gallatin County) denied Reier injunctive relief and later denied Reier's motion for relief from judgment.
  • Supreme Court of Montana: affirmed. Two justices dissented.

Issue

Is Reier entitled to an injunction to prevent Kramer from breaching the exclusivity provision of his personal service contract?

Holding

No. Reier was not entitled to injunctive relief. Its remedy is damages.

Reasoning

  • Personal services contract: performance depends on a particular person's skills, character, training and talents. Who performs matters more than what is promised (slides).
  • Traditional rule: courts will not grant affirmative specific performance of personal services, because compelling performance is difficult and looks like involuntary servitude (slides).
  • [Outside knowledge]: Montana statute bars an injunction to prevent breach of a contract whose performance could not be specifically enforced. Because Kramer's services could not be compelled, a negative injunction that would indirectly compel them was also unavailable. Damages were adequate.

Rule

Courts will not order specific performance of personal service contracts. Negative injunctions barring the employee from working for competitors are available, if at all, only in limited circumstances (e.g., unique services, an express negative covenant, and no undue hardship), and not where a statute or policy forbids indirectly compelling performance.

Why It Matters

  • It shows the personal-services limit on specific performance (compare Handicapped Childrens Education Board v. Lukaszewski Note 1).
  • Exam pattern: when an employee or performer walks, the employer gets damages (cost of replacement). A negative injunction is possible in some jurisdictions for unique talent with an express exclusivity clause. [Outside knowledge: Lumley v. Wagner is the classic example.]
