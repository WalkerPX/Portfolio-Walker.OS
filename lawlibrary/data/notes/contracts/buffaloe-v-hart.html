Citation: 114 N.C. App. 52, 441 S.E.2d 172 (1994) · Casebook: Knapp p. 373 · Topics: Statute of Frauds › UCC 2-201, UCC Article 2 - Scope and Formation One line: An oral $20,000 sale of five tobacco barns was enforceable under UCC § 2-201(3)(c). The buyer's check, though never cashed and later returned torn, was "payment accepted," and the buyer had "received and accepted" the barns.

Overview

The course case on the UCC statute of frauds exceptions for goods: part payment and receipt and acceptance (§ 2-201(3)(c)).

Parties

  • Plaintiff / appellee: Homer Buffaloe. A tobacco farmer who rented, then orally agreed to buy, the barns. He wanted damages for breach.
  • Defendants / appellants: Patricia Hart and Lowell Thomas Hart. Owners of the barns. They denied a contract and invoked the statute of frauds.

Facts

  • Buffaloe rented tobacco and five "roanoke box" tobacco barns from the Harts in 1988 on a "handshake" basis.
  • Late October 1988: he offered $20,000 for the barns, payable in four annual $5,000 installments. The Harts accepted with a handshake. He already had possession under the rental.
  • January 1989: he applied for a loan to buy the barns and listed them on his financial statement (the loan was denied). He and Mr. Hart reconfirmed the installment terms. He reimbursed the Harts for insuring the barns in 1989, which he would not have done as a renter.
  • October 1989: he advertised the barns for resale and took deposits from buyers ($500 from Mohorn, $1,000 from Stainback).
  • October 22-23, 1989: he delivered a $5,000 personal check to Mrs. Hart, noted as payment for the five barns. She offered a receipt, and he said "the check would be the receipt." The next night she said she would not sell, claiming she had sold them to someone else. The check was returned (slides: shredded) and never cashed.

Procedural History

  • Jury verdict for Buffaloe. The trial court denied the Harts' motions for directed verdict and JNOV.
  • Court of Appeals: no error (affirmed).

Issue

  1. Was there a sufficient signed writing under UCC § 2-201(1)?
  2. If not, did the buyer's check or his possession of the barns satisfy an exception under § 2-201(3)(c)?

Holding

  1. No. The check was not a sufficient memorandum under § 2-201(1), because the Harts never signed anything.
  2. Yes. The contract is enforceable under § 2-201(3)(c): (a) the seller's receipt of the check (holding it overnight before returning it) was payment made and accepted; and (b) the buyer received and accepted the barns, shown by his possession, his insuring them, and his attempts to resell them.

Reasoning

  • Goods: the barns were movable and identifiable, so they are goods under § 2-105, and the price exceeded $500. § 2-201 applies.
  • Writing: § 2-201(1) requires a writing signed by the party against whom enforcement is sought. Buffaloe's own check, signed by him, does not bind the Harts.
  • Payment exception: delivery and acceptance of a check can constitute payment, even if it is never cashed, if the seller accepts it. Mrs. Hart took the check and held it (citing Hofmann v. Stoller and Miller v. Wooters).
  • Receipt and acceptance exception: the buyer's conduct (possession, insurance, loan application, resale efforts) showed he had accepted the barns as owner, not as a renter.

Rule

Under UCC § 2-201(3)(c), an oral contract for the sale of goods of $500 or more is enforceable "with respect to goods for which payment has been made and accepted or which have been received and accepted." Tendering a check that the seller accepts can be payment even if it is never negotiated.

Why It Matters

  • It applies two of the five UCC § 2-201 exceptions (slides): (1) the merchant's confirmation / reply doctrine (§ 2-201(2)); (2) admission (§ 2-201(3)(b)); (3) specially manufactured goods (§ 2-201(3)(a)); (4) part payment (§ 2-201(3)(c)); (5) partial delivery / receipt and acceptance (§ 2-201(3)(c)).
  • Partial performance limits enforcement: with partial delivery, the contract is enforceable only for the goods accepted (slides: 10 of 100 units at $1,000 means $100 owed). Here the payment and acceptance covered the whole indivisible lot of barns.

Other Key Points

  • Hypo 3 (slides): Apex and BioSupply, 500 containers for $25,000 with a $5,000 deposit. Does the deposit satisfy (3)(c) for all the goods or only part?
  • Scope reminder: a "good" under § 2-105 must be tangible, movable and identifiable. A "sale" under § 2-106 passes title for a price. Gifts, leases and bailments are excluded.
  • Related: Jannusch v. Naffziger (Note 3 previewing the UCC statute of frauds).
