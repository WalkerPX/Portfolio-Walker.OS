Big picture

  • Reliance damages compensate a party for losses from reasonable reliance on a promise.
  • Purpose: put the promisee in the position he would have been in had the promise not been made.
  • They compensate losses from reasonable reliance on a promise that was ultimately broken.
  • Key use: appropriate when expectation damages cannot be proved with reasonable certainty (Wartzman, Note 1).

Doctrine outline

  • Reliance damages restore the pre-promise economic position.
  • Traditional limits on expectation recovery — foreseeability, certainty, mitigation, causation — may also apply to reliance-based damages (Wartzman, Note 3).
  • Promissory estoppel remedy: Restatement (Second) of Contracts — "the remedy granted for breach may be limited as justice requires" (Walser, Note 1).
  • Split of authority on the appropriate remedy for detrimental reliance cases (Walser, Note 1).
  • Restatement endorses a flexible approach to damages in promissory estoppel actions; Walser accepts this view, as do many recent decisions (Walser, Note 3).
  • If expectation damages cannot be proved with reasonable certainty, plaintiff is limited to reliance damages that can be established (Walser, Note 4).
  • Scholars debate how willing courts are to award expectation damages for promissory estoppel, and whether they should (Walser, Note 4).

Tests and elements

Checklist — reliance damages require:

  • A promise.
  • Reasonable reliance on the promise.
  • Loss suffered due to that reliance.
  • Foreseeability of the reliance (Tony/Cornelius hypo; Wartzman).
  • Causation (Wartzman: jury sufficiently instructed).
  • Mitigation not failed (Wartzman: plaintiffs not barred).
  • Certainty — if expectation damages cannot be proved with reasonable certainty, recovery limited to reliance damages that can be established (Wartzman Note 1; Walser Note 4).

Cases

  • Wartzman v. Hightower Productions (p. 1021) — Law firm failed to advise earlier that fundraising was covered by Maryland "Blue Sky" laws and required registration before stock could be sold to the public. When disclosed, principals lacked cash to hire a securities specialist or proceed; much of the money raised went into escrow. Enterprise abandoned. Principals recovered reliance damages equal to substantially all of their lost investment. Affirmed: jury sufficiently instructed on causation; foreseeability met; no bar for failure to mitigate; prejudgment interest properly denied.
  • Walser v. Toyota Motor Sales, U.S.A., Inc. (p. 1032) — Dealer mistakenly told he was approved as a Lexus dealer; in reliance, his father bought property for the new dealership. Promissory estoppel claim; jury found for plaintiff but limited damages to out-of-pocket expenses (reliance damages). Plaintiff appealed seeking lost profits. Affirmed: deal was "far from a certainty"; negotiations still preliminary; defendant tried to rectify the situation as soon as possible (informed plaintiff a couple days after the mistake); plaintiff failed to demonstrate loss of an opportunity by relying on the promise.

Examples and hypos

  • Tony/Cornelius: Tony promises $10,000 to buy a car; Cornelius relies and spends $9,000; Tony reneges. Foreseeable reliance → reliance damages of $9,000, putting Cornelius where he would have been had the promise never been made.
  • Delivery date: company promises delivery by a certain date; customer makes plans around it; company fails to deliver on time. Customer may get reliance damages calculated to restore the economic position before relying.
  • Employment: employer promises salary/benefits; employee accepts job and makes financial decisions based on the promise; employer reneges. Employee may be entitled to reliance damages.

Exam angles

  • When expectation damages fail on certainty, argue reliance damages (Wartzman).
  • Run the traditional expectation limits (foreseeability, certainty, mitigation, causation) against reliance claims (Wartzman Note 3).
  • Promissory estoppel remedy: cite the Restatement's "as justice requires" flexibility and note the split of authority (Walser Notes 1, 3).
  • To get lost profits in a reliance case, plaintiff must show a lost opportunity, not a deal "far from a certainty" (Walser).
  • Check mitigation and causation even in reliance recovery (Wartzman).
  • Foreseeability of reliance is a required showing (Tony/Cornelius hypo; Wartzman).
