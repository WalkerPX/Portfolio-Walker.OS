Quick reference

Citation: 246 Cal. App. 2d 123, 54 Cal. Rptr. 533 (1966) · Casebook: Knapp p. 606 · Topic: Duress and Undue Influence › Undue influence One line: A teacher pressured to resign immediately, while exhausted after his arrest, by officials who came to his home, stated a claim to rescind the resignation for undue influence, though not for duress, fraud, mistake or incapacity.

Overview

The course's model case on undue influence, and a model of careful doctrinal analysis. The court works through duress, menace, fraud, mistake and incapacity before finding that the facts state a claim for undue influence: excessive pressure on a person whose will is temporarily weakened.

Parties

  • Plaintiff / appellant: Donald Odorizzi. An elementary school teacher under contract for the next year as a permanent employee. He wanted his resignation declared invalid and to be reinstated.
  • Defendant / respondent: Bloomfield School District (acting through its superintendent and the school principal). It accepted the resignation and refused reinstatement.

Facts

  • June 10, 1964: Odorizzi was arrested on criminal charges of homosexual activity.
  • June 11: after arrest, questioning, booking and release on bail, and having gone about 40 hours without sleep, he was visited at his apartment by the superintendent and the principal. They said they were trying to help him and told him to resign immediately. There was no time to consult an attorney. If he didn't resign, he would be dismissed and the charges publicized, while resigning would let him find work elsewhere. [The detailed allegations are in the opinion; summarized.]
  • He signed the resignation. The District accepted it on June 13.
  • July: the criminal charges were dismissed. In September he sought to return, and the District refused.

Procedural History

  • The trial court sustained the District's demurrer and dismissed the amended complaint.
  • Court of Appeal: reversed (the complaint states a cause of action for undue influence). [Outside knowledge: reversed for further proceedings.]

Issue

Does the complaint state grounds to rescind the resignation for duress, fraud, mistake, incapacity or undue influence?

Holding

  • Duress / menace: no. Threatening to do what the District had a legal right to do (begin dismissal proceedings) is not duress.
  • Fraud / mistake: no. There was no actionable misrepresentation of fact.
  • Incapacity: no. He was not alleged to be so impaired as to lack understanding.
  • Undue influence: yes. The allegations state a claim.

Reasoning

  • Undue influence is "persuasion which tends to be coercive in nature, persuasion which overcomes the will without convincing the judgment." It involves (1) undue susceptibility of the weaker party (his exhaustion and distress), and (2) excessive pressure by the dominant party.
  • Indicators of excessive pressure (the Odorizzi factors, slides): (1) discussion at an unusual or inappropriate time, (2) consummation in an unusual place, (3) insistent demand that the business be finished at once, (4) extreme emphasis on the untoward consequences of delay, (5) use of multiple persuaders by the dominant side against a single servient party, (6) absence of third-party advisers, (7) statements that there is no time to consult financial advisers or attorneys.
  • Many of these were alleged: two officials at his apartment, immediate decision, no time to consult counsel, threats of publicity, and his exhaustion.

Rule

A contract is voidable for undue influence where a party who is unduly susceptible (temporarily or permanently) is subjected to excessive pressure or unfair persuasion by a dominant party, and that persuasion induces the agreement (Restatement (Second) § 177).

Why It Matters

  • Elements (Restatement, slides): (1) inducement (causation), (2) unfair persuasion (the Odorizzi factors), (3) undue susceptibility to domination, (4) an inequitable result.
  • Note 1: a confidential relationship isn't required under the Restatement, but the relationship must justify assuming the other won't act inconsistently with one's welfare (§ 177(1)).
  • Note 2: not all factors are required. Look at the totality.
  • Note 3: duress may be harder to prove because it requires a wrongful threat.
  • Note 4: lack of good faith is not an element of undue influence. Odorizzi could rescind even if the officials honestly thought they were acting for the school system.
  • Week 7 Part 2 slides suggest undue influence may also be the best explanation for Syester v. Banta.
