The law of finders allocates rights when true owners lose or mislay personal property.

A. Universal Common Law Rule

A finder of personal property acquires title good against all the world except the true owner (and prior lawful possessors).

B. Classification of Found Property

  1. Lost Property:
    • Definition: Property unintentionally and involuntarily parted with by the true owner through neglect or inadvertence.
    • Right: Belongs to the finder against all except the true owner (even if found on another's private land, unless the finder is a trespasser or employee).
  2. Mislaid Property:
    • Definition: Property intentionally placed in a specific spot by the true owner who subsequently forgets to retrieve it.
    • Right: Belongs to the owner of the premises (locus in quo), not the finder. The premises owner holds the item as a bailee, increasing the likelihood that the true owner will return to reclaim it.
    • Key Problem: Candy at Garcia's Restaurant — A purse left on a dining booth seat is mislaid property; the restaurant owner (Garcia) has superior possessory right over the finder (Candy).
  3. Abandoned Property:
    • Definition: Property voluntarily and intentionally relinquished by the true owner with no intent to reclaim.
    • Right: Belongs entirely to the first finder who reduces it to possession.
