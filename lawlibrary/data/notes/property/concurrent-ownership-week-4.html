A. Joint Tenancy (JT)

  • Core Feature: Right of Survivorship — upon the death of one joint tenant, their share automatically passes to the surviving joint tenant(s). Nothing passes by will or intestacy.
  • The Four Unities (PITT):
    1. Possession: Equal right to possess the whole.
    2. Interest: Equal undivided shares and identical legal estates.
    3. Title: Acquired by the same instrument or joint adverse possession.
    4. Time: Acquired at the same time.
  • Severance: Conveyance of a joint tenant's interest inter vivos destroys the unities of Time and Title, severing the JT as to that share and converting it into a Tenancy in Common.

B. Tenancy in Common (TIC)

  • Core Feature: Default co-ownership estate. No right of survivorship.
  • Characteristics:
    • Each tenant owns an undivided, fractional share of the whole.
    • Shares may be unequal.
    • Interests are fully alienable inter vivos, devisable by will, and inheritable.
    • Only Unity of Possession is required.

C. Tenancy by the Entirety (TBE)

  • Core Feature: Concurrent estate reserved exclusively for married couples with right of survivorship.
  • Requirements: Five Unities — Time, Title, Interest, Possession, and Marriage.
  • Protection: Neither spouse can unilaterally convey, encumber, or sever the property without the other's consent. Individual creditors of one spouse cannot attach TBE property in most jurisdictions.
