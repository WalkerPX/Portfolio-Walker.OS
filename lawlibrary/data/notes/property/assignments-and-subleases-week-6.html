Distinguishing Assignments and Subleases (Week 6)

The legal relationship between Landlord (L), Tenant (T), and Transferee depends on whether the transfer is an assignment or a sublease.

A. The Common Law Rule

  • Assignment: T transfers the entire remaining lease term to Transferee (A), retaining no reversionary interest.
  • Sublease: T transfers less than the entire remaining term to Transferee (S), retaining a reversionary possessory right.

B. Privity Relationships

Relationship DimensionPrivity of Contract (P/K)Privity of Estate (P/E)Who Can Landlord (L) Sue for Rent?
Assignment (T → A)Retained between L and T.Established between L and Assignee (A).L can sue T (via P/K) OR sue A (via P/E).
Sublease (T → S)Retained between L and T.Exists between T and S. NO P/E between L and S.L can sue ONLY T. L cannot sue S for rent.

Complex Multi-Transfer Problem Analysis (Week 6)

  • Fact Pattern: L leases to T for 10 years. T transfers to T1 for 2 years (Sublease). Later, while T1 is in possession, T assigns "entire remaining interest" to T2. T1 defaults on rent.
  • Liability Analysis:
    • L can sue T based on Privity of Contract (P/K never ends unless novation occurs).
    • L can sue T2 based on Privity of Estate (T2 holds the ultimate reversionary fee assignment).
    • L cannot sue T1 directly for rent without an express assumption contract.
