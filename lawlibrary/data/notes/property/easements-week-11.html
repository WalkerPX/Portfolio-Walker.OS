Types of Servitudes (Week 11)

  1. Easement: Non-possessory property right to use land of another for a specific purpose.
  2. License: Revocable permission to use another's land (e.g., ticket holder, guest).
  3. Profit: Right to enter land and remove natural resources (timber, minerals).

Creation of Easements by Operation of Law (Week 11)

  • Express Easement: Created in writing satisfying Statute of Frauds.
  • Implied by Prior Use (Quasi-Easement):
    1. Common owner severs tract into parcels.
    2. Pre-existing continuous prior use by original owner before severance.
    3. Continuation of use is reasonably necessary.
  • Implied by Necessity:
    1. Common owner severs tract into parcels.
    2. Severance creates strict necessity for access (landlocked).
    3. Terminates automatically when necessity ends (Othen v. Rosier).
  • Prescriptive Easement:
    • Requires Actual, Open/Notorious, Adverse/Hostile (without permission), and Continuous use for statutory period.

Scope and Division of Easements (Week 11)

  • Appurtenant Scope: An appurtenant easement cannot serve land outside the original dominant estate (Brown v. Voss).
  • One Stock Rule (Miller v. Lutheran Conference): Co-owners of an easement in gross must act as "one stock" (unanimous agreement required for licensing/use). Restatement 3rd allows division unless contrary to intent or creates unreasonable burden.

Negative Easements (Week 11)

  • Right of dominant owner to prevent servient owner from doing specific acts on servient land.
  • Traditional English Four Categories:
    1. Blocking windows (light).
    2. Interfering with air flow in defined channel.
    3. Removing building support (excavation).
    4. Interfering with artificial watercourse flow.
