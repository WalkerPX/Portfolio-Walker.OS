Property rights are not natural or self-defining; they reflect specific legal, political, and historical choices made by courts and sovereign authorities.

A. Discovery and Conquest

  • Core Rule: Discovery and conquest confer legal title to the discovering sovereign, giving that sovereign the exclusive power to extinguish indigenous possessory claims and convey land.
  • Key Precedent: Johnson v. M'Intosh — Demonstrates that property rights are legal constructs defined by the conquering sovereign's court system rather than pre-existing absolute rights.

B. First Possession

  • Core Rule: Being "first" in time is a foundational legal principle, but courts define what specific type of "firstness" qualifies for legal ownership.
  • Application: Simply encountering or sighting property first does not automatically confer ownership. Courts require specific acts of domain, capture, or control.

C. Capture of Fugitive Resources

  • Core Rule: Mere pursuit of a wild animal or fugitive resource (e.g., oil, gas, wild game) does not create legal title. Title requires actual physical capture or depriving the resource of its natural liberty.
  • Majority Rule (Pierson v. Post): Requires actual capture, mortally wounding, or securing the wild animal in nets/traps. Pursuit alone, even with hounds, creates no legal right. Valued for certainty and bright-line administration.
  • **Dissent (Livingston, J.)**: Argues that labor, investment, and public policy incentives (e.g., encouraging the destruction of noxious beasts) should be rewarded even prior to physical capture.

D. Pre-Possessory Interest

  • Core Rule: When an individual takes significant but incomplete steps to achieve possession of abandoned/unowned property and that effort is unlawfully interrupted by the tortious act of another, the actor acquires a qualified pre-possessory interest.
  • Key Precedent: Popov v. Hayashi — Where a fan attempted to catch Barry Bonds' historic home run ball but was tackled by a crowd, the court recognized a pre-possessory interest, ordering a partition by sale and split of proceeds between the pre-possessory claimant and the eventual retriever.
