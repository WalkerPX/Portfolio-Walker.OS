Classification of Leasehold Tenancies (Week 5)

A. Term of Years

  • Definition: Estate bound by a fixed, predetermined beginning and ending date.
  • Creation: Must be in writing if the term exceeds the Statute of Frauds threshold (typically > 1 year).
  • Termination: Terminates automatically at the agreed ending date; no notice required.
  • Death Effect: Death of landlord (L) or tenant (T) does NOT terminate the lease; obligations bind the estate.

B. Periodic Tenancy

  • Definition: Leasehold that continues for repeating successive periods (e.g., month-to-month, year-to-year) until proper notice is given.
  • Creation: Express agreement or implied by rent payment intervals.
  • Termination: Requires advance written notice:
    • Year-to-Year: 6 months' notice under common law (1-3 months in modern statutes).
    • Month-to-Month: Full period advance notice (typically 30 days).
  • Death Effect: Death of L or T does NOT terminate; lease continues repeating until personal representative gives notice.

C. Tenancy at Will

  • Definition: Leasehold enduring at the mutual agreement of L and T, with no fixed period or rental interval.
  • Termination: Terminates at will by either party instantly (modern statutes usually require 30 days' notice).
  • Death Effect: Automatically terminates upon the death of either L or T.

D. Tenancy at Sufferance (Holdover)

  • Definition: Arises when T lawfully enters possession but wrongfully remains ("holds over") after lease expiration.
  • Landlord Remedies:
    1. Eject T as a trespasser and recover damages.
    2. Elect to hold T to a new periodic tenancy based on prior rental terms.

Professional Identity & Practical Judgment (Week 5)

  • Landlord-tenant disputes require integrating legal doctrine with practical client representation.
  • Lawyers must communicate legal risks clearly to clients, evaluating realistic cost-effective solutions alongside formal litigation remedies.
