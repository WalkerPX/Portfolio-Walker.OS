Comprehensive Property Law Final Exam Outline

Unit 1: Acquisition of Property & Finders Law (Weeks 1–2)

1. Modes of Property Acquisition

  • Discovery and Conquest:
    • Rule: Discovery/conquest gives legal title to discovering sovereign; sovereign has exclusive power to extinguish indigenous claims and convey land.
    • Precedent: Johnson v. M'Intosh — Property rights reflect legal, political, and historical choices of sovereign courts.
  • First Possession & Capture:
    • Rule: "Firstness" is court-defined. Pursuit alone is insufficient; actual physical capture, mortal wounding, or control is required for fugitive resources (Pierson v. Post).
    • Dissent (Livingston, J.): Favors rewarding labor, investment, and public policy incentives over strict physical capture.
  • Pre-Possessory Interest:
    • Rule: When significant but incomplete steps toward possession are unlawfully interrupted by another's tortious act, actor holds a qualified pre-possessory interest (Popov v. Hayashi — home run ball split).

2. Common Law of Finders

  • General Rule: Finder acquires title valid against all the world except the true owner and prior possessors.
  • Classifications:
    • Lost Property: Unintentionally and involuntarily dropped. Belongs to finder against all except true owner.
    • Mislaid Property: Intentionally placed, forgotten. Belongs to owner of premises (locus in quo) to protect true owner's return (Candy v. Garcia's Restaurant).
    • Abandoned Property: Intentionally relinquished. Belongs to first possessor.

3. Adverse Possession (AP)

  • Elements: (1) Actual entry/possession, (2) Open and notorious, (3) Hostile/adverse (under claim of right, without permission), (4) Exclusive, (5) Continuous for Statute of Limitations (SOL).
  • Effect: SOL expiration bars ejectment; AP gets fee simple title relating back to entry date (Day 1).
  • Tacking & Privity: Successive possessors combine time periods ONLY if in privity (voluntary transfer via deed, will, or valid oral transfer).
  • Color of Title: Invalid written instrument gives constructive adverse possession over entire described tract.

Unit 2: Estates, Future Interests & Concurrent Ownership (Weeks 2–4)

1. Present Estates & Future Interests

  • Fee Simple Absolute (FSA): Potentially infinite duration ("to A and his heirs" / "to A").
  • Life Estate (LE): Duration measured by A's life ("to A for life"). Reversion in O or Remainder in 3P.
  • Life Estate Pur Autre Vie: Measured by life of another ("to A for the life of B").
  • Term of Years: Leasehold estate for fixed duration ("to A for 20 years"). Reversion in O.

2. Defeasible Fees & Grantor Future Interests

  • Fee Simple Determinable (FSD): Durational language ("so long as", "until"). Reverts automatically to O via Possibility of Reverter.
  • Fee Simple Subject to Condition Subsequent (FSSCS): Conditional language ("provided that", "but if"). Reverter is optional; O retains Right of Entry / Power of Termination (requires re-entry).
  • Fee Simple Subject to Executory Limitation (FSSEL): Reverts automatically to Third Party (3P) via Executory Interest.

3. Concurrent Ownership & Partition

  • Joint Tenancy (JT): Right of Survivorship. Requires Four Unities: Possession, Interest, Title, Time (PITT). Inter vivos conveyance severs JT into Tenancy in Common.
  • Tenancy in Common (TIC): Default co-ownership. No survivorship. Fractional alienable, devisable, inheritable shares. Unity of Possession only.
  • Tenancy by the Entirety (TBE): Marital co-ownership (PITT + Marriage). Right of survivorship; no unilateral conveyance or creditor attachment.
  • Partition:
    • Partition in Kind: Default physical division into separate parcels.
    • Partition by Sale: Forced sale and division of net proceeds when physical division is impracticable.

Unit 3: Landlord-Tenant Law (Weeks 5–6)

1. Types of Tenancies

  • Term of Years: Fixed start/end date. Writing required if > SOF threshold. Terminates automatically without notice. Death of L or T does not terminate.
  • Periodic Tenancy: Repeating periods (month-to-month). Requires period-length notice (6 mos for year-to-year at common law). Death of L or T does not terminate.
  • Tenancy at Will: Endures at mutual will. Terminates instantly by either party or automatically upon death of L or T.
  • Tenancy at Sufferance (Holdover): Wrongful holdover T. L may eject or hold to new periodic tenancy.

2. Assignments vs. Subleases & Privity

  • Assignment: T transfers entire remaining term. Creates Privity of Estate (P/E) between L and Assignee (A). L and T remain in Privity of Contract (P/K). L can sue T (P/K) or A (P/E) for rent.
  • Sublease: T transfers less than entire term. P/E and P/K remain between L and T; P/E exists between T and Subtenant (S). NO P/E between L and S; L can sue ONLY T for rent.
  • Assumption Agreement: If Assignee/Subtenant assumes lease covenants in writing, L can sue directly as Third-Party Beneficiary (3PB) under P/K.

Unit 4: Real Estate Transactions & Executory Period (Weeks 7–8)

1. Four Stages of Real Estate Transactions

  1. Listing Agreement (Seller & Broker duties).
  2. Sales Contract (Purchase & Sale agreement).
  3. Closing (Deed delivery & title transfer).
  4. Post-Closing (Deed covenants govern).

2. Statute of Frauds (SoF) & Contract Remedies

  • SoF Requirements: Writing, signed by party to be charged, property description, essential terms (parties, price, closing date).
  • Remedies:
    • Expectation Damages: $ ext{Contract Price} - ext{Fair Market Value at Breach}$.
    • Rescission & Restitution: Deposit retention/return.
    • Specific Performance: Mandatory conveyance based on uniqueness of land.

3. Executory Period & Marketable Title

  • Equitable Conversion: Contract execution gives Buyer equitable title; Seller retains legal title as security.
  • Risk of Loss: Buyer bears casualty risk under majority rule (unless seller negligent or contract allocates otherwise).
  • Marketable Title: Implied covenant to deliver title free from reasonable doubt/encumbrances at closing.
  • Duty to Disclose: Modern rule requires seller to disclose known material latent physical defects.

Unit 5: Title Protection, Deeds & Recording Acts (Week 10)

1. Deed Types

  • General Warranty Deed: Warranties title against all defects arising before OR during grantor's ownership.
  • Special (Limited) Warranty Deed: Warranties title ONLY against defects arising during grantor's ownership ("by, through, or under Grantor").
  • Quitclaim Deed: Zero warranties; grantee assumes all title risk.

2. Recording Acts & Notice Types

  • Notice Statute: Subsequent BFP (value + no notice) wins regardless of who records first.
  • Race-Notice Statute (e.g., Indiana IC 32-21-4-1): Subsequent BFP wins ONLY if BFP takes without notice AND records first.
  • Pure Race Statute: First to record wins regardless of notice.
  • Notice Types: Actual, Constructive (properly recorded), Inquiry (inspection/document recitals).
  • Shelter Rule: Transferee taking from BFP steps into BFP's shoes and receives BFP protection.

Unit 6: Servitudes — Easements, Real Covenants & CICs (Weeks 11–13)

1. Easements

  • Appurtenant vs. In Gross: Appurtenant benefits dominant estate & runs automatically; In Gross benefits person/entity.
  • Creation: Express (SoF writing), Implied from Prior Use (quasi-easement + reasonable necessity), Implied by Necessity (severance + strict landlocked necessity), Prescriptive (actual, open, hostile, continuous).
  • Scope & Division: Appurtenant easement cannot serve non-dominant land (Brown v. Voss). One Stock Rule applies to co-owned easements in gross (Miller v. Lutheran Conference).

2. Real Covenants vs. Equitable Servitudes

  • Real Covenants (Money Damages): Burden requires Writing, Intent, Touch & Concern, Horizontal Privity, Strict Vertical Privity, Notice. Benefit requires Writing, Intent, Touch & Concern, Relaxed Vertical Privity (No Horizontal Privity required).
  • Equitable Servitudes (Injunction): Burden requires Writing (or common subdivision scheme), Intent, Touch & Concern, Notice. NO Horizontal or Vertical Privity required.

3. Common Interest Communities (CICs)

  • Condo vs. Coop: Condo = fee simple unit + TIC common areas; Coop = corporate building owner + shares + proprietary lease.
  • CC&Rs Standard: Recorded master covenants presumptively valid unless arbitrary or against public policy (Nahrstedt).

Unit 7: Public Land Use Regulation & Constitutional Protections (Weeks 13–14)

1. Zoning Policy & Land Use Controls

  • Trade-Offs: Protection against incompatible uses vs. Exclusionary zoning, housing cost inflation, and economic/racial segregation.
  • Nonconforming Uses & Vested Rights: Vested rights protect development after substantial commitments; Estoppel applies to detrimental reliance on permits; Amortization provides transition period for nonconforming uses.

2. Constitutional Foundations

  • Fifth Amendment Takings Clause: Bars uncompensated physical takings or regulatory takings.
  • Due Process (5th & 14th Amendments): Protects against arbitrary or retroactive land use regulations.
  • Equal Protection (14th Amendment): Bars discriminatory zoning classifications.
