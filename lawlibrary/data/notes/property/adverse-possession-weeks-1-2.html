Introduction to Adverse Possession (Week 1)

Adverse possession converts wrongful occupancy into lawful fee simple title through the passage of time.

  • Core Mechanism: When an adverse possessor occupies land continuously for the statutory limitation period, the original owner's cause of action for ejectment/trespass is barred.
  • Doctrine of Relation Back: Once the Statute of Limitations expires, the adverse possessor acquires a brand-new legal title that relates back to Day 1 of their original entry. Original owner's title is completely extinguished.

Mechanics of Adverse Possession (Week 2)

Adverse possession transforms a trespasser into a fee simple owner after the expiration of the statutory period of limitations (SOL).

A. Five Essential Common Law Elements

  1. Actual Entry and Possession: Gives the true owner notice of trespass and triggers the cause of action.
  2. Open and Notorious: Possession must be visible and obvious to an ordinary inspection of the land, giving constructive notice.
  3. Hostile and Adverse (Under Claim of Right): Occupancy must occur without the owner's permission.
  4. Exclusive: Possessor holds land for themselves, not sharing possession with the true owner or general public.
  5. Continuous for Statutory Period: Possession must be as continuous as a typical owner's use throughout the full SOL.

B. Title Consequences & Relation Back

  • Extinguishment: Upon SOL expiration, O's ejectment and trespass claims are forever barred, extinguishing O's legal title.
  • Relation Back: AP's new title relates back to the very first day of entry (Day 1).
